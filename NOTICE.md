# Attribution and release scope

Bibliographic records originate from the U.S. National Library of Medicine's PubMed service. Records retain PMID and DOI links where available. Source publication titles, metadata and third-party rights remain with their respective sources. The availability of a PubMed record does not provide a blanket license to redistribute a publisher's abstract or full text. This release excludes those abstracts and raw source documents. See [NCBI policies](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/home/about/policies/) and [E-utilities guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK25497/).

Available citation metrics originate from dated NIH iCite snapshots. Missing metric values remain missing. See [NIH iCite](https://icite.od.nih.gov/).

Embeddings were computed using AllenAI's SPECTER2 base model and proximity adapter. The model cards identify the upstream Apache 2.0 license; model weights are not distributed here. See [SPECTER2](https://huggingface.co/allenai/specter2) and [SPECTER2 base](https://huggingface.co/allenai/specter2_base).

The topic hierarchy uses graph partitioning, keyphrase extraction with [Toponymy](https://github.com/TutteInstitute/toponymy), and assistant-reviewed topic names. Its labels and representative-paper descriptions are exploratory annotations. The release does not assert expert validation or scientific novelty.

The repository's MIT license applies to its original software and documentation. The project's rights in its computed embeddings, coordinates, neighbor arrays and topic assignments/annotations are licensed under CC BY 4.0, as described in [LICENSE-DATA.md](LICENSE-DATA.md). Neither license grants rights over source publications, bibliographic metadata, pretrained models or dependencies. Dataset files retain their source attribution and processing provenance; no blanket license is asserted over third-party content.

The release excludes credentials, machine-specific configuration, private reading lists, research notes, raw source texts and pretrained model caches. Data hashes identify the published artifacts; they are not a substitute for the original papers when evaluating a scientific claim.
